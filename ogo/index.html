OffGridOperator

Most solar software tells you what the panels made. This one computes what they should have made — from solar geometry and the measured sky — then finds out why they didn't, and settles the day in dollars.

01

It knows what the sky did

Measured global irradiance, cloud cover and air temperature, logged alongside every array's output. The weather is data, not an excuse.

02

It knows what the panels should have made

Plane-of-array expectation computed from solar geometry and the measured irradiance — so a shortfall is a real capture loss, never “it was cloudy”.

03

It knows why they didn't

Each array-hour is classified: full sun, shaded, under-producing, or diffuse-limited. Shade you can plan around. A fault you fix. Diffuse light you accept.

04

It prices the gap

The day settles as a profit-and-loss statement — what solar earned, what it left on the table, and which array lost it to what.

Straight answers

What it doesn't do yet

A system that hides its limits is asking you to find them yourself. These are stated in the product too — the screens above show them.

Curtailment is detected, not priced

Classifying each array-hour as full, shaded, under-producing or diffuse ships and is visible above. The priced curtailment ledger does not — the P&L panel says so itself, and reads $0.00 drawn. The detection is real; the money attribution for curtailment is still to come.

The capture grades are provisional

Panel tilt and azimuth have not been surveyed at this site, so the expectation uses a flat-plane estimate and every grade is badged accordingly. The ranking between arrays holds; the absolute percentages will move when the angles land.

It runs on limited hardware

One production site, one set of inverters, charge controllers and battery management. The modelling is general; the integrations are not. Extending it to other equipment is the obvious next job.

The portal is not public

It runs on the LAN of the house it operates, without an authentication layer, because so far it has had exactly one user. That is why this page exists — screenshots of a real system rather than a login you cannot get past.
